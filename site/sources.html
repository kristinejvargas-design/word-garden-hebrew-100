FIRST 100 · TRANSPARENCY

Sources & method.

The list is computed from source text and lemma tags—not copied from a commercial vocabulary deck.

How the list was counted

  1. Every verse word in the Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible (OSHB) Westminster Leningrad Codex files was counted under its numbered Strong’s lexical head. Short attached prefixes stay grouped with the base lemma. Unnumbered grammatical affixes are not ranked as separate lemmas; proper names remain in the list.
  2. Entries are sorted by descending count. The run contained 300,007 verse tokens with a numbered Strong’s lemma and 8,640 distinct Strong’s IDs; the top 100 account for 51.6% of that counted set. This is a lemma-token statistic, not a claim about English-word coverage or reading fluency.
  3. Pointed Hebrew, transliteration, and glosses come from the Open Scriptures Hebrew Lexicon’s Strong’s data. Each KJV example is a verse containing the tagged lemma in the source Hebrew text.

Primary sources

  • Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible (morphhb) — the WLC base text is public domain; OSHB lemma and morphology annotations are CC BY 4.0. Attribution: “Original work of the Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible available at https://github.com/openscriptures/morphhb”.
  • Open Scriptures Hebrew Lexicon — Strong’s Hebrew forms, transliteration, and gloss data. The lexicon data is CC BY 4.0; the underlying Strong’s dictionary text is public domain.
  • Arul John’s King James Bible JSON — verse text used for the KJV examples. The KJV translation is public domain; this repository is distributed under the MIT License.
  • Logos: “The 7 Best Books for Learning Hebrew Vocabulary” (October 5, 2026) — recent category context for frequency lists, roots, vocabulary-in-context, and the “50×” threshold.
  • Public search results for “learn biblical Hebrew vocabulary” — current snippets commonly surface frequency decks, active recall, spaced review, roots, and Bible-context practice. Search result order varies by location and time.

The “50× words” description summarizes Logos’ article: its account of a vocabulary-in-context resource covers 642 words occurring 50 or more times (excluding proper names) and reports approximately 90% Old Testament coverage. That is a separate resource and a different list from First 100.
